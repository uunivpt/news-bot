OUR EDITORIAL METHODS

How PoliticsHub sources, labels and corrects stories

Our public policy explains source attribution, the limits of automated summaries and how readers can request a correction. The original publisher link remains part of the story.

What to check before sharing a headline

Start with the publication date and the original reporting organisation. A source headline can be accurate about an early development and still be outdated after an official statement or court order. For elections, compare provisional trends with the declaration for the exact constituency. For public policy, identify whether a statement is a proposal, a notification or a rule that has taken effect.

PoliticsHub displays source links so readers can inspect the original context. Our news feed is not a substitute for an official record or an independent fact-check. We keep editorial methods and a correction route public. Readers can explore the news source directory, consult the our editorial standards or request a correction. These steps help separate what a source reported from what has actually been confirmed.

How to read and verify our news coverage

PoliticsHub brings together India news, Maharashtra coverage, politics, world affairs and public-affairs explainers. Some headlines are short summaries based on attributed publishers, while our guides explain the institutions and records behind recurring stories. A short source-linked brief is not the same thing as an independently investigated report. When an article links to another newsroom, open that original publisher's page to understand its complete context, timestamps, supporting documents and any subsequent corrections.

For elections, pay attention to the difference between an early counting trend, a projected winner and an official declaration. A claim from a candidate or political party should be attributed to that speaker until supported by independent or official evidence. The same distinction matters when authorities announce a new policy: a draft, a ministerial statement, a Gazette notification and an operative rule are not interchangeable. Our election-results guide and government-notifications guide explain how readers can check these details without relying on a headline alone.

Dates, locations and institutional names can change the meaning of a report. A story about one Maharashtra constituency should not be treated as a statewide result; a decision affecting one department should not be presented as a national rule. If reporting develops after publication, check the most recent original source rather than sharing an old screenshot. We encourage readers to keep the original link with their posts, to distinguish confirmed information from evolving claims, and to avoid turning uncertain developments into definitive statements.
