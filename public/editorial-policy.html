Editorial Policy

Our editorial principles for sourcing, attribution, corrections and automated newsroom processing.

Accuracy first

We aim to distinguish verified facts, attributed claims and analysis. We link to original sources where available and avoid presenting a source's claims as independently verified facts when they are not.

Attribution

Source names and source links are displayed where available. Headlines and summaries should not remove important context from the underlying report.

Automation and editorial value

PoliticsHub.in uses deterministic software to collect, normalize and compare newsroom material. A source-derived brief is never presented as independent reporting. Where multiple distinct sources are available, the system can add factual cross-source curation by identifying agreement and source-specific details. Single-source material remains source-linked and is not represented as original reporting.

Before a report is described as reviewed

Review must check the final article against accessible source documents, distinguish allegations from established facts, verify quotations and dates, and retain supporting references. A source link alone does not establish that a source supports the claim. We must not invent reporting, quotations, sources or reviewer identities.

Original work and rights

An attributed excerpt or automated summary is not, by itself, independent reporting. An article considered for advertising must add useful original commentary, analysis or curation, with an editorial review of the actual text. Word counts and software eligibility checks do not certify originality or compliance. Attribution is not permission to reproduce copyrighted text or images; reuse requires an applicable licence, permission or other lawful basis.

Originality, copyright and AI-assisted work

We do not claim a software plagiarism or AI-detection score proves originality. Before promoting a source-derived report as original or enabling advertising, editorial review should compare the final wording and claims against its source, confirm evidence and image rights, and identify substantive independent context. Republishing, lightly paraphrasing or mechanically spinning articles from another publisher is not original reporting. Where a brief only summarizes source coverage, it is labeled as source-linked and is not eligible for ads without a separate documented review.

AI-assisted drafting, if used, must be checked against accessible primary sources, with uncertain claims and quotations verified or removed. Staff must not fabricate interviews, authors, permissions, dates, or credentials. Avoid publishing unauthorized pirated content or content that promotes illegal or harmful activity. Public-interest reporting about conflict, crime, abuse, or controversial policies is not automatically banned; context, accuracy, protection of minors and victims, and platform advertising restrictions require case-by-case assessment.

Advertising and sensitive coverage

Source-only briefs, utility pages and reports withheld for verification do not carry advertising. Sensitive stories require separate review for Google Publisher Policies and applicable restrictions before advertising is enabled. Editorial review must consider harmful or misleading claims, graphic material, privacy, and the treatment of victims and children. Advertising eligibility is separate from whether a story is newsworthy.

Withheld reports

If a published report lacks sufficient supporting evidence, its URL may show a clear review notice while the text, advertising and article-discovery entries are withheld. Material corrections are recorded through our corrections policy.

Conflicts

Paid or sponsored material must be clearly identified and must not be presented as independent editorial reporting.
