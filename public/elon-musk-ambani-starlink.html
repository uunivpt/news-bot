Elon Musk targets Mukesh Ambani over Starlink's India launch delay

In a sarcastic X post addressed to “Prime Minister Ambani,” the SpaceX chief alleged that powerful commercial interests are obstructing Starlink. India's government rejects claims of preferential treatment and points to pending security and spectrum requirements.

PoliticsHub News Desk · Updated 10 October 2026, 1:23 AM IST · 5 min read

NEW DELHI, 10 October 2026: Elon Musk escalated his public criticism of the delay in Starlink's commercial launch in India on Friday, 9 October. In a message published on X, Musk sarcastically addressed Reliance Industries chairman Mukesh Ambani as “Prime Minister Ambani” and accused him of benefiting from restrictions on competition. The accusation is Musk's claim, not an established finding.

“Dear Prime Minister Ambani”

In the post, Musk argued that his satellite broadband service could help people in areas with limited internet connectivity, including schoolchildren and small businesses. He asked whether Starlink would be allowed to compete in the Indian market. His use of the title 'Prime Minister' was a sarcastic remark aimed at Ambani; India's actual prime minister is Narendra Modi.

What triggered the dispute?

Starlink, operated by Musk's SpaceX, has been seeking to start commercial satellite internet services in India. Although the company has obtained key preliminary authorisations, additional national security checks and spectrum-allocation requirements still need to be completed before customer services can begin. Earlier in the week, Musk questioned whether business interests were contributing to the delay, without publicly presenting evidence that Reliance had interfered with the approvals process.

India's official response

Communications Minister Jyotiraditya Scindia rejected the suggestion that India permits monopoly protection in satellite communications. The government has said that Starlink, Reliance Jio's satellite unit and Eutelsat OneWeb are at broadly comparable regulatory stages, with security checks under way and spectrum pricing being finalised. Holding an initial licence does not itself authorise commercial rollout.

But didn't Jio and Starlink announce a partnership?

Yes. Reliance Jio and SpaceX announced a distribution agreement in March 2025 that envisioned offering Starlink equipment in India, subject to regulatory approvals. That commercial arrangement and the companies' potentially competing satellite broadband interests can coexist. The existence of the agreement does not, by itself, prove or disprove Musk's allegation of interference.

What is confirmed, and what remains unverified?

Confirmed: Musk publicly posted the remarks on 9 October 2026; Starlink had not yet begun commercial service in India; and government officials have publicly attributed the remaining delay to required approvals and spectrum arrangements.

Not established: No evidence made public in the cited reporting demonstrates that Mukesh Ambani or Reliance directed regulators to delay Starlink, nor that its eventual market launch date has been determined. PoliticsHub will update this report if official approvals, company statements or independently documented evidence change the situation.

Why the story matters

The dispute raises two distinct public policy questions: whether satellite broadband operators should have fair opportunities to compete and how regulators should verify security compliance before allowing services to operate. Consumers in poorly connected regions have an interest in both reliable internet access and appropriate safeguards. The price and affordability of any future service remain important to its practical impact.
